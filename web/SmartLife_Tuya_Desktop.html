Connection

Tuya's cloud API is called straight from your browser. The US and EU endpoints accept direct browser calls; the China endpoint (and some networks) need a CORS proxy in between.

Leave empty for a direct connection. Use {url} in the text if your proxy expects the target address as an encoded query parameter.

Stream gateway & cameras

Cameras you add (✎ Edit → ＋ Add camera) stream over RTSP, which browsers can't play directly. Point this page at a small stream gateway on your network — go2rtc or MediaMTX — and the stream plays right in this page: inside each camera tile (when previews are on) and in the ▶ Watch player. The gateway needs a stream with the same name as the camera here, fed from the same RTSP address (the card's Copy URL button gives you the exact address). Leave the address empty to hand streams to an external player app (such as VLC) instead. (The Windows edition needs no gateway for tiles — it decodes them natively with a bundled ffmpeg.)

Show the gateway's feed inside each camera tile. Turn off to keep tiles compact.

Help

How login works. This page signs in to Tuya's cloud with the same account you use in the Smart Life (or Tuya Smart) app, then asks Tuya for your device list and sends control commands — the same approach as the classic Smart Life web dashboards. Pick the region where your account was registered (the country you chose when you created the account in the app); the wrong region is the most common cause of “login failed”.

Your credentials. Your username and password go directly to Tuya's servers (px1.tuya*.com) over HTTPS. If you tick “Keep me logged in”, they are kept in this browser's local storage so the app can silently sign back in when the session token expires — use it only on your own devices, and use “Forget saved login” above to erase them.

The proxy. Browsers block some cross-site calls. The US and EU Tuya endpoints allow direct calls from web pages, so no proxy is normally needed there. The China endpoint doesn't, so in “Auto” mode CN traffic is routed through a community CORS proxy (cors.smartathome.co.uk). You can pick a different proxy or your own above — note a proxy can see the requests it forwards (they contain a session token, never your password after sign-in… the sign-in request itself does contain your password, so only use proxies you trust for CN logins).

Customising. Sections sort A–Z by name by default. Use ✎ Edit to drag devices into your own order inside a section (that order then sticks, and each section has a “Reset to name order” button), create groups (rooms), rename devices with an alias, or hide devices you never use. Use the 📌 button on any card to pin a shortcut copy of it in the Pinned section — the device also stays in its group below. Scenes always live in their own Scenes section. Sections appear in the order Cameras → Pinned → Lighting → your groups → Ungrouped → Scenes by default; in Edit mode every section header has ↑ / ↓ buttons to move the section itself up or down, and your arrangement is remembered too. Everything is remembered in this browser.

Lighting & group controls. Lights organise themselves: any bulb or strip that isn't assigned to one of your groups lives in the built-in Lighting section, and cameras get their own built-in Cameras section the same way — assigning a device to a group always wins over the automatic sections. The Lighting header (and the header of any group containing a light) has group controls: a master switch (on if any member is on; clicking turns the whole group on or off), brightness and colour-temperature sliders showing the members' average, and colour swatches. Group controls broadcast — they send the command to each member in turn, and changing a single light afterwards simply overrides it for that light. Nothing about the group itself is stored.

Cameras (RTSP). Tapo cameras: in the Tapo app, open the camera → Settings → Advanced Settings → Camera Account and create a camera account, and make sure RTSP is enabled for the camera. The username and password you add here are that camera account — not your TP-Link cloud account. Then use ✎ Edit → ＋ Add camera and enter the camera's IP address (a fixed or DHCP-reserved IP works best, so the address doesn't change). The app builds the stream address for you: rtsp://user:pass@IP:554/stream1 (high quality) or /stream2 (standard) — or pick “Custom path” for other RTSP cameras. Camera cards sit alongside your devices and can be pinned, grouped and reordered just like them; unassigned cameras live in the built-in Cameras section — the first section by default — until you assign them to a group of your own. In Edit mode each camera card has a Size choice: Normal (one grid cell), Large (two columns wide) or Wide (the full row), and the video area follows the card's width. Their passwords are stored in this browser with the same light obfuscation as the saved login.

Watching a stream. Web browsers can't play rtsp:// directly. Without a gateway, ▶ Watch hands the stream address to a player app installed on your device (such as VLC) — or use Copy URL and paste the address into VLC's “Open Network Stream”. For in-page playback, run a small stream gateway such as go2rtc or MediaMTX on your network, give it a stream with the same name as the camera here (fed from the same RTSP address — Copy URL has it), and enter the gateway's address in Settings → Stream gateway & cameras: the feed then plays inside each camera tile and in the ▶ Watch player. (The Windows edition of this app decodes RTSP itself with a bundled ffmpeg — no gateway needed there.)

Limits. Tuya rate-limits how often the device list can be fetched and how fast commands can be sent. When that happens this page quietly keeps showing the last known states, retries commands a moment later, and stretches its refresh interval until Tuya is happy again — no action needed. Autorefresh every 30s or slower is comfortable.
